# Acceptors, admission gate and sprint math — reference

Source: «SEO Монстр 2027» ch6 pp. 471–496, 539, 545–548.

## Acceptor registry — six fields (pp. 471–472)

1. Cocoon role: Target L1, Mixed L2, Support L3.
2. Query cluster and Primary Entity.
3. 28-day GSC baseline: impressions, clicks, CTR, average position.
4. Next user action after landing: lead form, cart, calculator, template download, Mother-link click to a commercial node.
5. Internal redistribution pages: Daughter-links down, Mother-links up.
6. Trust contour status: verified author/reviewer, legal details, local NAP, proof blocks (cases, tables, reviews).

Permanent ID per acceptor in Materials & Acceptors. Judge acceptors by their job routing the signal, not by felt importance. Missing any field → not an acceptor.

## Admission gate — four buckets (pp. 478–479)

| Bucket | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 1. Ready growth point: indexed, top 20, structured | proceeds to readiness checks; goal after links: top 1–3 |
| 2. CTR / post-click trust deficit | snippet, price, terms fixes first |
| 3. Cannibalizing group (isSmearedSignal) | merge or role-separate first |
| 4. Isolated / unfinished node | strictly banned from external links; MVC of 6–9 pages first |

## 7-mode failure matrix (pp. 488–489)

1. Low CTR inside the top 15 → inspect top-5 SERP, rewrite title/description around a concrete number or artifact, add table markup. Do not build links.
2. Clicks but no conversions → price, calculator, specialist, guarantees, working CTA on first screen before links.
3. Internal cannibalization → 2–3 URLs alternating for one query in Query-Page → 301 merge or strict entity/anchor separation.
4. Isolated URL, no cluster → build Minimum Viable Cluster first.
5. Baseline trust deficit → E-E-A-T/NAP work first.
6. Thin, LLM-reproducible content → Data-Shift rebuild: proprietary tables, `<dl>` specs, cases, boundary conditions.
7. Scaled-content junk at section level (mass geo-clones, junk filter pages) → stop generating; purge with 410, noindex or 301 before any links.

## 6-item admission checklist (pp. 492–493)

1. Single clear Job to Be Done, no mixed formats.
2. Assigned cocoon role.
3. Passed Index Selection with stable GSC impressions.
4. No competing duplicate in Query-Page.
5. Working next step on screens 1–2.
6. Full E-E-A-T contour matching niche risk.
(plus: neighbor cluster pages link in contextually and can redistribute LinkValue; user can close the whole session on the domain within one click — price, process, criteria, limits, calculator, address, booking.)

Reject at ≥2 failed items or any failure mode. Natural-pretext test: write one non-promotional reason an outside author would cite the URL; one-sentence asset test: what unique table/figure/calculation the reader gets. YMYL/e-commerce: also check responsible-person transparency, legal details, return rules, contacts, expert review.

Numbers: 10–20 candidates/month; ready zone positions 1–20; fast-win zone 8–18; low-CTR diagnosis zone top 15, inspect top 5.

## Readiness card (pp. 494, 545–548)

Technical card (8 items): HTTP 200; Googlebot-crawlable; no accidental noindex or login wall; clean final URL; valid canonical matching Google's choice; mobile parity; indexable main content; dated and attached to the placement task.

Integration (5 items): reachable through navigation; supporting internal links; connects to related guides/methods/tools; leads directly to the service; no broken key elements.

Conversion (8 items): test lead/order submitted and confirmed by sales; phone works; price correct; stock correct; terms stated; confirmation shown; tracked in analytics; CRM receives it.

AI-citation structure (asset pages): 40–60-word answer capsule; raw-HTML table/dl; stated applicability limits; methodology + primary-source links; author and reviewedBy with sameAs; `<time datetime>`; identical figures across blocks and schema.

Pre-distribution check: live canonical URL; files reachable; no access restriction; mobile OK; author and contacts; internal links; forms/tools work; expert available.

Scoped pauses: broken URL; section under migration; broken funnel; missing promised asset; spam campaign; all expansion during hack or manual action. Each pause: owner + checkable resume criterion. Re-run the technical card if a lot of time passes between approval and publication.

## Sprint math (pp. 473–476, 483–484, 488–491)

- Cluster depth around a commercial hub: 4–6 Mixed L2 + 10–15 Support L3.
- Layer split: up to 50–60% of placements to citable L2/L3 assets (calculators, compatibility tables, guides, templates).
- Sprint volume: 5–8 placements per month into one cluster (e.g. 2→L1, 3→L2, 2→L3), or 1 category + 3–5 connected pages per wave.
- Test batch: 2–3 links on a commercial page before scaling; watch for cluster-leader shift.
- Mother-link placement: first third of L3 body text.
- Young domain: hostAge <1 year → reinforce homepage with branded/URL/neutral anchors only, after it has body-text links to priority L1s.

Homepage hub audit: body links to L1s; block order matching revenue; sitewide news clutter removed; non-commercial anchors only.

Category readiness: scenario presets; short selection criteria; links to subcategories and whitelisted static filters; filled specs on the first screen.

Service readiness: named specialists; geography and delivery format; transparent price or calculator; quantified cases and verified reviews; risk/warranty FAQ; cocoon apex position.

Anti-patterns: spreading links thin (1 each across 8 sections); 80–90% of links on the homepage; commercial anchors on homepage/price list/broad category; links to a bare product grid; everything at the homepage to "concentrate weight".

## Link Target Readiness Analyzer tiers (pp. 723, 729–731)

Full prompt in seo-prompt-kit. Output: tier A (reinforce now) / B (after on-page fixes) / C (build cluster pages first) / D (do not reinforce); authority-transfer map; roadmap; four 0–100 scores (Link Readiness, AI Citation Readiness, Topical Authority Potential, Entity Reinforcement); Link ROI rating (low/medium/high/very high); top-20 improvements. Judge whether the page deserves link equity — never by its link count.
